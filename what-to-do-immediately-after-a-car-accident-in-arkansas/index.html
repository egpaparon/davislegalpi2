What to do immediately after a car accident in Arkansas

March 21, 2025 · By Shannon Davis

Car accidents can happen in an instant, leaving you shaken and unsure of what to do next. Whether it’s a minor fender-bender or a serious collision, knowing the right steps to take immediately after an accident is crucial—especially in Arkansas.

Acting quickly and correctly can protect your safety, strengthen your insurance claim, and ensure you comply with local laws.

Failing to follow the proper procedures could lead to legal complications, delays in insurance payouts, or even missed opportunities for compensation.

This guide will walk you through the essential steps to take after a car accident in Arkansas, helping you stay safe, organized, and legally protected.

Stay Calm and Ensure Safety

The moments after a car accident can be chaotic, but staying calm is critical. Panicking can lead to poor decisions, so take a deep breath and focus on the following:

– Turn on your hazard lights to alert other drivers.

– Move to a safe location if possible (e.g., the shoulder of the road or a nearby parking lot).

– Check yourself and passengers for injuries—some injuries, like whiplash or internal trauma, may not be immediately apparent.

If the accident is severe and vehicles are immobile, stay inside with your seatbelt on until emergency responders arrive.

Call Emergency Services

In Arkansas, you are legally required to report an accident if there are injuries, fatalities, or property damage exceeding $1,000. Even if the crash seems minor, calling 911 ensures that police document the incident, which is vital for insurance claims.

For serious injuries, call 911 immediately.

The police department will file an accident report, which could serve as crucial evidence later on. If an officer doesn’t arrive, you can file a report to the Police Department within 30 days.

Exchange Information

After ensuring everyone’s safety, exchange the following details with the other driver(s):

– Full name and contact information

– Driver’s license number

– Insurance company and policy number

– Vehicle details (make, model, license plate)

Important: Be polite but do not admit fault—even saying “I’m sorry” could be used against you later. Stick to the facts when speaking with the other driver and police.

Document the Scene

Gathering evidence at the scene can significantly strengthen your insurance claim or legal case. Use your phone to:

– Take photos of vehicle damage from multiple angles.

– Capture road conditions, traffic signs, and skid marks.

– Record weather and lighting conditions.

– Get contact information from witnesses (their statements could be valuable later).

If possible, write down your own account of the accident while details are fresh in your mind.

Seek Medical Attention

Some injuries, like concussions or internal bleeding, may not show symptoms right away. It’s best to get checked by a medical professional as soon as possible.

A medical report also creates an official record linking your injuries to the accident, which is essential for insurance claims.

Report the Accident to Your Insurance Company

Notify your insurance provider as soon as possible—most companies require prompt reporting. Provide them with:

– The police report number

– Photos and documentation from the scene

– Medical records (if applicable)

Avoid giving a recorded statement until you’ve consulted with an attorney, as insurers may use it to minimize your claim.

Follow Up on Your Claim

Keep track of all accident-related expenses, including:

– Medical bills

– Vehicle repair costs

– Lost wages from missed work

Follow up with your insurance adjuster regularly and keep copies of all communications. If your claim is denied or underpaid, an attorney can help appeal the decision.

Consult with an Arkansas Car Accident Attorney

Dealing with insurance companies can be stressful, especially if the other driver disputes fault. A local Bentonville car accident attorney can:

– Investigate the accident to determine liability.

– Negotiate with insurance companies for fair compensation.

– Help file a lawsuit if necessary.

Arkansas follows a fault-based system, meaning the at-fault driver’s insurance should cover damages. However, insurers often try to reduce payouts, making legal guidance invaluable.

A car accident can be overwhelming, but taking the right steps immediately afterward can protect your health, finances, and legal rights.

By staying organized and proactive, you can navigate the aftermath of an accident with confidence.
