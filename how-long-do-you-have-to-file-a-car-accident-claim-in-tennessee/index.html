How Long Do You Have to File a Car Accident Claim in Tennessee?

April 20, 2026 · By Shannon Davis

After a car accident, most people are focused on recovering physically, dealing with vehicle repairs, and navigating insurance communications.

Legal deadlines are often the last thing on their mind. But in Tennessee, missing the deadline to file a car accident claim can permanently eliminate your right to seek compensation, no matter how clear-cut your case may be.

Understanding how long you have to file a car accident claim in Tennessee is not just a technical detail. It is one of the most important pieces of information you need to protect your legal rights after a crash.

The Tennessee Statute of Limitations for Car Accident Claims

In Tennessee, the statute of limitations for personal injury claims arising from a car accident is one year from the date of the accident. This means you must sue the at-fault driver within a year of the crash.

One year may sound like a reasonable amount of time, but it passes quickly when you are managing medical treatment, dealing with insurance companies, and rebuilding your life after a serious collision.

Many accident victims are caught off guard by how soon the deadline arrives, particularly if they spent the early months of recovery assuming the insurance process would resolve the matter without litigation.

If you miss the one-year deadline, Tennessee courts will almost certainly dismiss your case regardless of the merits, and you will lose your right to pursue compensation through the court system entirely.

Property Damage Claims

It is worth noting that the deadline for property damage claims in Tennessee differs from the personal injury deadline.

If you are seeking compensation for damage to your vehicle or other personal property, you have three years from the date of the accident to file a claim.

However, relying on this longer window as a reason to delay is not advisable.

The evidence, witnesses, and documentation that support your property damage claim are the same materials that support a personal injury claim. Waiting too long to take action can compromise the quality and availability of that evidence.

Why the Deadline Matters Even If You Are Negotiating With Insurance

A common misconception is that the statute of limitations only applies if you plan to go to court. In reality, the deadline is crucial even during insurance negotiations, as they can collapse at any moment.

If the insurance company is dragging out the claims process or making lowball settlement offers, you may eventually need to file a lawsuit to protect your interests.

If the one-year deadline has already passed by the time negotiations fail, you have lost your leverage entirely. The insurance company knows this, and some adjusters will intentionally delay proceedings until the deadline has expired.

Filing a lawsuit or retaining an attorney before the deadline does not mean your case will necessarily go to trial. It merely safeguards your legal options and guarantees that you approach negotiations with strength, not desperation.

Exceptions That May Affect the Deadline

Tennessee allows for limited circumstances to extend or pause the standard one-year deadline.

If the injured party is a minor at the time of the accident, the statute of limitations may not begin running until they reach the age of majority.

If the at-fault driver left the state of Tennessee after the accident and before a lawsuit could be filed, that time away from the state may not count toward the deadline.

In cases where injuries were not immediately apparent and could not reasonably have been discovered right away, the clock may begin running from the date of discovery rather than the date of the crash.

These exceptions are narrow and fact-specific. Do not assume an exception applies to your situation without consulting an attorney who can evaluate the details of your case.

What Are the Implications When a Government Entity Is Involved?

If your accident involved a government vehicle or occurred on poorly maintained public property, different rules apply.

Claims against government entities in Tennessee are subject to the Tennessee Governmental Tort Liability Act, which has its own procedural requirements and shorter notice deadlines.

In some cases, you may be required to provide formal notice of your claim within a matter of months. Missing these notice requirements can be just as fatal to your claim as missing the standard statute of limitations.

Why You Should Act Well Before the Deadline

While the law gives you one year, waiting until the deadline approaches to take action is a significant risk. Evidence degrades over time. Witnesses move away, forget details, or become difficult to locate. Surveillance footage is overwritten. Physical evidence at the scene disappears.

Medical records become harder to connect clearly to the accident as time passes.

Retaining an attorney early allows them to immediately begin preserving evidence, identify all potentially liable parties, and build the strongest possible case on your behalf.

Starting the process promptly also gives your legal team time to negotiate thoroughly with insurers rather than rushing to meet a court filing deadline.

Your Time Is Limited. Your Rights Are Worth Protecting.

Understanding how long you have to file a car accident claim in Tennessee is the first step. Taking action within that window is the next one.
